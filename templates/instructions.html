What we'll ask about

Four kinds of questions. Not the questions themselves — just the shape of them.

Motive

Why did things happen the way they did — and who ended up better off?

Hint: look for the decisions that got made and who signed off on them. If someone benefited, it's usually implied rather than stated outright.

Connections

Different people sometimes notice different pieces of the same pattern. Some questions only make sense once you connect two things you read separately.

Hint: two people can describe what looks like separate, ordinary observations while actually pointing at the same underlying issue from different angles.

Timeline

Pay attention to when things happened, not just what happened. The order and the gaps between events can matter.

Hint: notice when someone first raises a concern versus when the organization formally responds. The size of that gap can be meaningful on its own.

Distinguishing threads

Not everything that sounds similar is part of the same story. Some things that look connected turn out to be separate.

Hint: overlapping names or close timing don't automatically mean two things are related. Check what each document is actually about before assuming a link.
