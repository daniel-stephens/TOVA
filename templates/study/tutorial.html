Nothing has started yet

A quick tour before you start

Two minutes of reading now will save you more than that later. The clock has not started — it starts when you press the button at the bottom of this page.

1 · The clock

You have {{ task_minutes }} minutes with the tool

When the time runs out the tool closes and the questions begin. You can also press Finish early whenever you feel ready — there is no advantage to waiting if you are done.

If you need to stop and come back, the clock pauses. Sign out, close the laptop, lose your connection — none of it costs you time. You get {{ task_minutes }} minutes of actually using the tool.
2 · Themes

The circles are groups of related documents

A computer read every document and sorted them into groups it thought belonged together. Each circle is one group. Circles that sit close together hold documents that resemble each other.

Click a circle to see what is in that theme, the words that define it, and how confident the computer is about it.

The groupings are a guess, not the truth. They were made by a computer that has not read for meaning the way you will. Treat them as a starting point, and expect to disagree with some of them.
3 · Naming

You can rename a theme, or ask for a suggestion

Open a theme and press the small pencil next to its name. You can type whatever you like — a theme called "Topic 7" is much easier to remember as "safety complaints".

Suggest asks the system to propose a name for that theme, based on the documents in it. If you do not like the one it gives you, press it again for a different one — there is no limit.

Nothing is saved until you press Save. Press Cancel, or just close the theme, and the name stays exactly as it was.

A suggestion is only a suggestion. It comes from a machine reading the same documents you can read, and it can be wrong or bland. Your own name for a theme is usually the more useful one.
4 · Documents

Every document is in the list on the right

  • Search finds words anywhere in any document. This is usually the fastest way to chase something specific — a name, a date, a phrase you half-remember.
  • Theme filters the list down to one group.
  • Score is how strongly a document belongs to its theme. A low score often means the computer was unsure, which can be a document worth reading.

Click any row to read the whole document.

5 · Reading

Opening a document shows you why it was grouped that way

The full text is on the left. On the right is the theme the computer put it in and the keywords behind that decision, so you can see the reasoning rather than just the result.

6 · Disagreeing

If a document is in the wrong theme, move it

Pick a better theme from the list, or choose New theme… and type your own name if nothing in the list fits. Press Save.

This changes what you see. It does not affect other participants, and you can always put it back by picking the original theme again.

Renaming and moving things is genuinely useful, not just permitted. If "Topic 7" is really about safety complaints, calling it that will help you remember it later — and you will be answering from memory.

7 · Active learning

You can label documents one at a time

Your version of the tool has an Active learning button in the top right. It gives you a queue of documents and asks you to label each one, starting with the ones the computer is least sure about.

The keyboard is quicker than the mouse here: press 1–9 to pick a suggestion, Enter to save and move on, ← and → to go back or skip, and / to jump to the search box.

Labelling is optional. Use it if it helps you make sense of the collection; ignore it if it does not.

{% endif %}

If you remember nothing else

  • Search is your fastest tool. Reading everything in {{ task_minutes }} minutes is not possible, and is not the point.
  • Read the documents themselves, not only the theme names. The questions afterwards are about what the documents say.
  • Rename and move things as you go. Your own labels are much easier to remember than "Topic 7".
  • You answer from memory. When the tool closes you cannot reopen the documents — you will only see their numbers and subject lines to point at.
