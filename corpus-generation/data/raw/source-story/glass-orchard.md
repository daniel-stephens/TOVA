# The Glass Orchard

This source is an original film production, not a retelling of an existing movie. The documents to generate are short pieces about two fictional films that share people, places, and ideas.

## The films

Mara Voss directed the short *Night Window* ten years ago. A cracked glass pear sits on a kitchen table while a lullaby plays, and a will leaves a family orchard to the younger sister. The older sister denies the will was ever read.

Her new feature, *The Glass Orchard*, is in post-production at Halden Pictures. On the page it is a story about two sisters, a fire, and the same orchard. The festival print is due at the Northline Film Festival. The last reel, the fire, is missing from the print the studio sent. Dailies of that reel exist. The lead, Ivo March, tells a magazine the fire was never shot.

## People

- Mara Voss, director of both films. She talks about the orchard as fiction.
- Ivo March, lead actor. He gives interviews that contradict the dailies.
- Lena Cho, editor. She has the missing reel and the lullaby stems.
- Paul Ettin, composer. He reused the *Night Window* lullaby in the feature score and hopes nobody names the source.
- Ruth Adler, Northline programmer. She is choosing the festival cut.
- Samir Shah, Halden producer. He wants the fire out of the film and the family resemblance buried.

## What the pieces know, separately

No single note should explain the whole link. A dailies comment can show the glass pear. An interview can deny the fire. A score memo can mention a lullaby without naming *Night Window*. A programmer's note can say the missing reel changes the ending. A producer's email can say the will in the script is "too close to Mara's family" without quoting the short. A review can notice that the lullaby, the pear, and the restored fire are one idea only if the reader has seen the other pieces.
